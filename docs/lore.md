# SINBOUND: lore bible

Owned by the story role (docs/team/story.md). Read this before you write any flavour text, item
names or enemy descriptions, so everything speaks with one voice. The tone is dark, dry and a
little funny. We are the villains, and we know it.

## The three worlds
- **The Silver City** (Heaven), above. Ruled in practice by the **Archons**, who stand on the steps
  of the Throne and relay its commands. Its soldiers are the Host: Choir Wisps, Dawn Lancers,
  Seraph Sentinels, Ophanim, Censer Clerics, and the Archons of the Seventh Gate.
- **The Verge**, in between. The mortal world, now a battlefield of grey plains, ruined sanctums
  and graveyards. Every run takes place here.
- **The Pit** (Hell), below. Cold and getting colder.

## The premise
The Archons have proclaimed the **Last Dawn**: a sunrise that will never set. Once it has fully
risen, no shadow will survive in any world, and Hell is made of shadow. Its bearer is
**Sariel, Herald of the Last Dawn**, a six-winged seraph who descends on the Verge to light it.

Hell is also starving. Heaven has strung the **Lattice of Grace** across the sky, a net of light
that catches souls before they can fall. (The **Penitent Statues** on the Verge are souls caught
halfway down, frozen into a repentance they never chose.)

The **Ashen Court** answered by tearing open **the Breach**, a wound up into the Verge, and
sending up the **Sinbound**: demons whose sins are too heavy for them to stay dead. When a
Sinbound is slain, the weight of their sin drags them back down into the **Font of Brimstone** at
**Basecamp Hell**, the war camp on the lip of the Breach, where they are made again. This is the
roguelite loop, and it exists inside the fiction. Dying is called being **Unmade**.

## Hell's factions
- **The Ashen Court**: the dukes of the Pit, on thrones of cooled slag. They ordered the war and
  never come up to fight it. Voice: **Duchess Malphine**, who speaks only through letters.
- **The Ninth Forge** (Brimstone Foundry): smiths who quench steel in sinners' tears. Eight
  Forges before it died trying to make an Archon-killing blade. Voice: **Grimmaul**.
- **The Black Choir**: fallen angels who still sing, but backwards. Voice: **Vesper Ashtongue**,
  whose tongue burned to ash the first time she sang the hymn in reverse.
- **The Ledger** (House of Mammon): the accountants of damnation, where every soul is a debt.
  Voice: **Mammon's Clerk**, who sold his own name for a chair at Mammon's table.
- **The Fallen Roads**: scouts and wayfarers who walk the paths the angels once came down by.
  Voice: **Wayfarer Ossek**.
- **The Chainward**: jailers of the deepest pit (Skarn's order).
- **The Unshriven**: imps, husks and rabble. Voice: **Pip**, courier of the Ashen Court.

## The arc: the Empty Throne
Story beats unlock across runs, one new conversation per NPC per Basecamp visit (Hades-style).
Beats are flags (`beat.*`) that become common knowledge (`known.*`) at the next run start.

**Act I. The Breach** (runs 1 to ~4). Meet the camp, learn the war, kill the Herald.
**Act II. The Hollow Hymn** (`beat.hollow_hymn`, around run 4+).
1. Grimmaul: angel steel has been forged in a hurry. Heaven was never in a hurry (`brittle_steel`).
2. Vesper: every hymn is a question sung toward the Throne. None of them come back answered.
3. The Herald's death lines start to doubt ("The light... it was supposed to answer").
4. Vesper: where does the Lattice put the souls it catches? Not in Heaven (`lattice_question`).
5. The Clerk: the soul accounts no longer balance. Someone is spending souls (`lattice_answer`).
6. The Clerk: the Lattice souls are burned as fuel for the Last Dawn (`dawn_fuel`).
7. The Grand Inquisitor appears. Heaven is purging its own doubters; Ossek finds a seraph hanged
   by its wings with a sign reading DOUBTED.

**Act III. The Empty Throne** (`beat.empty_throne`, around run 10+, 3+ boss kills).
Vesper's reveal: the Throne is empty. It may have been empty since the Fall, which may simply
have been the moment it emptied. The Archons rule from the steps, pretending. Hell remembers the
Fall, and every demon is a witness that once there was Someone to fall from. **The Last Dawn is
not a cleansing. It is a pyre to burn the witnesses, lit with the souls of the dead.**

The demon's answer is a villain's: *"Then the chair is free."* From here on the war is a march
on the **Seventh Gate** (`beat.march`). Grimmaul forges a crown; the Clerk prepares an audit of
Heaven; Pip discovers Malphine plans to destroy the Sinbound once they have cleared the way
(`path.defy_court`). The ending is meant to be the demon climbing the stairs to the empty chair.

## Style rules for writers
- Angels are "it". Demons have names and pronouns.
- No modern slang, no swearing. Menace through calm, humour through understatement.
- The narrator is the Pit itself: second person, short sentences, italics, never jokes.
- Keep lines short (under ~160 characters) so they fit the box and barks.

## Act IV. The Ending and the Reconquest
The march ends at the **Seventh Gate** above the Sunlit Citadel. With two of its guardians
felled, the Gate opens. Past it there are stairs, and Archons who kneel "to the habit of
kneeling". At the top is a very large, very plain, very empty chair with dust on the armrests.
The demon chooses:
- **Sit**: shadows lengthen across every world.
- **Break it**: there is no Throne any more, only whoever is strongest.

Either way, the Last Dawn gutters out, the Lattice falls slack, souls fall again and the Pit
grows warm. Heaven is headless, not dead. Archons who would not kneel rally the
**Reconquest**, and runs go on as the defence of the new order. Grimmaul's crown sits on a
pillar by the Basecamp bonfire.

## Bonds (affinity)
Offerings at Basecamp reveal each NPC's secret:
- Grimmaul talks to his first anvil, "Darling".
- Vesper sings you a single note of the backwards hymn.
- The Clerk half-remembers his lost name.
- Ossek was once a scout of the Host.
- Pip was once a forgotten white lie.
- Kesh's father went up to see the sky and never came back.

## Heaven's champions (bosses)
- **Sariel**, Herald of the Last Dawn: Lilaeth's former choir-sister; it begins to doubt.
- **Ozias**, the Grand Inquisitor: burns doubters, including angels. His sermons end in fire.
- **Ysmir**, the Frost Abbot: prayed in the snow for forty years and called the silence holy.
- **Aurelion**, the Dawn Wyrm: hatched from the first sunrise; the Last Dawn's hearth.
- **Mother Verdance**, Saint of the First Garden: tends a garden no one walks in any more.
- **Azrafel**, Archon of the Seventh Gate: the last warden. It knew the Throne was empty and
  guarded it anyway. Its death line asks you to forgive it for what you will find.
